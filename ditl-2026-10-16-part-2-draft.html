DITL, 15 to 25 Oct 2026, Part 2 (draft): first real bets, closing-line value, and NASCAR¶

Short summary. Part 1 ended with nothing proven. Part 2 is where the work meets real books and an honest yardstick. On 16 Oct we bet a sportsbook's US Grand Prix race-winner board on the calibrated F1 model (6 of its 7 lines, $9.80). We bet six cycling races (28 bets, $169.20). We also measured, for the first time, whether our backtest trades beat the market's closing price (closing-line value, CLV), and whether our model beats Kalshi on NASCAR. So far, with one misclick and two off-model tickets taken out, three cycling stages settled for +$58.69 on $90.00, and the model's own lines made +$32.69 of that on $80.00. Three stages say little either way. F1 calibration raises backtest profit in every strategy-season but not on the three-weekend holdout. On NASCAR, Kalshi prices race wins more sharply than we do. This is a working draft: from here it adds only the work that locks in our Austin strategy and bets, and it will be finished after the race on Sun 25 Oct. The remaining cycling and Phoenix results are follow-ups for a later part.

All times are UTC. Sportsbooks are named only by letter (A, B, C). Stakes on the ₮ (USDT) book are shown as $. Nothing here is betting advice.

Part 1 (13 to 14 Oct): where we started

Part 1 (13 to 14 Oct) covered cycling bets, Austin preparation and our first Probalytics tests. It left us with:

  • A calibration fix for the flat F1 model. It was tested on two seasons of recorded prices but never used on a live board.
  • No closing-line value anywhere. Our go/no-go goal for October (G1) is CLV above +2% over 100 live bets, and nothing measured it yet.
  • Evidence that our backtest's one-price fills flatter thin markets. Probalytics' full-depth order books showed it.
  • A rule from 15 Oct: every remaining F1 round is paper-traded on one strategy locked before the first session, and scored as bet.

What ran

  • The Austin board (16 Oct, 04:00 to 04:40). Sportsbook B's 22-line race-winner board came in nine days before the race. We priced it on production data with the walk-forward calibration power applied (a = 2.1). Once that setting reached production (04:36), production priced the board itself, and the two agreed within 3.2 points on every driver. The slip bets every line with at least 5% expected value against the calibrated model, at half Kelly on a $100 bank, rounded up to the nearest 10 cents with a $1 minimum.
  • CLV in every backtest (16 Oct, 05:06 to 05:45). Every saved season sweep now records CLV per strategy. We ran the calibrated model off and on for 2025 and 2026 on Polymarket's recorded prices: 44,000 backtest bets, every one with a closing price.
  • Cycling (15 to 17 Oct). Eneco Tour stage 3, Tour of Guangxi stages 4 and 5 and its overall winner, the Japan Cup and the Chrono des Nations, priced on our road model. An experimental stage-aware model was tested and kept off.
  • Fill fidelity (14 to 16 Oct). The F1 backtest re-run with fills at the ask on Probalytics' order books, on both Polymarket and Kalshi.
  • NASCAR (16 Oct). The same calibration fitted for NASCAR on 158 races, swept against Kalshi's recorded prices, and a Sportsbook A board for Sunday's Phoenix race priced against both.

A. New data, model updates and calibration

New data this part. Kalshi's recorded NASCAR prices for 42 races (1,328,148 trades, 351,032 prices) and Polymarket's for 20 (too thin to use). Probalytics' full-depth order books for the 2026 F1 season on Polymarket and Kalshi, used in C. And closing-line value (CLV) now saved with every season sweep: 44,000 F1 backtest bets, every one with a closing price.

F1: does the calibrated model beat the close?

How CLV is measured. Entry is the price we paid, as the YES price, with costs and fees. Close is the exchange's price at the start of the race that decides the market (qualifying for pole). Polymarket uses the last trade within 6 hours, and Kalshi the book mid within 10 minutes. CLV in points is close minus entry for a YES position, the other way round for NO. CLV in percent divides that by the price paid. Means are per bet, not weighted by stake. Points is the steadier read, because percent over-weights cheap contracts.

What this says: - Profit. In-sample, calibration raises backtest profit for every strategy in both seasons: 18 of 18 strategy-seasons. - CLV. It moves less than profit: up for every 2025 taker strategy, mixed in 2026. - Share beating the close. Fewer than half of any taker strategy's bets beat the close (42 to 51%). At 16 to 33 bets per strategy, that is noise either way. - Log loss. The model gets closer to Polymarket with calibration but stays behind it in 2025; in 2026 it edges ahead.

Cycling: log loss, because there is no CLV

CLV is not available for cycling: there is no exchange and no captured closing odds. We report log loss instead. A stage-aware model beat our baseline on 2026 stage races, but its prices run too close to 50%. The one-day version was no better, so neither is in use.

C. Probalytics fill fidelity: what survives real order books

Backtest profit for the update taker strategy on F1 2026, in-sample, on Probalytics' order books:

Polymarket Kalshi
Our recorded prices, one price per order +$1,096 +$1,096
Probalytics' order books, fills at the ask +$1,415 +$25
… plus a sale floor (never sell below our fair price minus the edge) +$2,031 +$1,470
… walking the book instead, with the floor +$1,918 +$1,607

At the ask, Polymarket's in-sample edge survives and Kalshi's almost disappears. Most of Kalshi's loss was the strategy selling into wide bids whenever our fair price dipped: a sale floor alone brings it back. Walking the book adds about nothing once the floor is on. All of this is in-sample and averaged over three seeds. On three held-out 2025 weekends the floor made things worse on Polymarket (−$37 to −$72 at the ask), so it is not adopted: it stays an option, off by default, and our live signals don't use it. Kalshi has no held-out test yet.

The verdict: the new pipeline (Probalytics' tape, fills at the ask) is more faithful because it removes the optimism of filling at the mid price, but none of the strategy changes we tried (spread limit, calibration, book walk, sale floor) shows an edge out of sample. On three held-out 2025 weekends, fills at the ask showed no edge on either tape.

Full detail: the fill-fidelity report has every run, the book-walk bug and its fix, the 2025 holdout and what's next.

What this does and doesn't show

  • It shows our first real bets placed on a calibrated model, and the first CLV measurements on our backtests.
  • It shows calibration helps F1 backtest profit in-sample, and that Kalshi out-prices our NASCAR model.
  • It doesn't show that any strategy makes money. Without the misclick, the settled profit is +$58.69 over three stages, most of it from one stage's matchups. Every 2026 backtest number is in-sample, and the holdout is three weekends.
  • It doesn't show live CLV yet. That arrives with Austin, and it is what the +2%-over-100-bets goal is measured on.
